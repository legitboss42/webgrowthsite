# Small Business Website Launch QA Checklist

A website launch should not feel like crossing your fingers and hoping the important parts still work.

For a small business, launch day affects trust, lead flow, search visibility, and first impressions immediately. If the homepage is live but the form fails, if the service pages look broken on mobile, or if the redirects were skipped, the website can start losing value on day one.

This small business website launch QA checklist is designed to help you catch the problems that matter most before and after go-live. If you want a broader planning version, also read the [Website Launch Checklist for Small Businesses](/blog/website-launch-checklist-for-small-businesses/). If you need hands-on help before publishing, the [website maintenance service](/services/website-maintenance/) or [website audit service](/services/website-audit/) is the direct implementation path.

## What launch QA is actually for

Launch QA is not a cosmetic exercise.

Its real job is to reduce avoidable commercial risk. That includes:

- broken enquiry flow
- poor mobile usability
- missing trust pages
- wrong redirects
- weak metadata
- missing analytics events
- broken internal links
- slow or unstable key pages

If the website exists to support calls, bookings, WhatsApp enquiries, quote requests, or search visibility, then launch QA is part of revenue protection.

## Pre-launch gate: do not go live if these fail

Before launch, confirm the following high-risk checks:

1. Contact forms send to the correct inbox.
2. The thank-you state or success message appears after submission.
3. The main CTA buttons point to live destinations.
4. Phone, email, WhatsApp, and booking actions work on mobile.
5. Homepage and primary service pages load acceptably on a real phone connection.
6. About, Contact, Privacy, Terms, Disclaimer, and Editorial Policy pages are accessible.
7. Title tags, meta descriptions, canonicals, and robots intent are set correctly on key indexable pages.

If one of these fails, the safest decision is usually to delay launch and fix it first.

## Homepage and conversion QA

The homepage is often where launch weaknesses show up fastest.

Before go-live, check:

- the hero clearly explains what the business offers
- the primary CTA is visible without deep scrolling
- the copy does not overpromise or sound generic
- trust signals appear early enough to reduce doubt
- service links are easy to find
- contact paths are repeated near decision points
- the page does not feel heavy, cluttered, or confusing on mobile

If a homepage looks expensive but still leaves visitors guessing what to do next, it is not launch-ready.

## Service-page QA

Every important service page should answer the buyer's basic questions clearly.

Before launch, review whether each main service page explains:

- who the service is for
- what problem it solves
- what happens in the process
- what is included
- what makes the offer credible
- what the next step is

Also test whether the service page links naturally to:

- related guides
- relevant proof or portfolio material
- the contact or review request path

Weak service pages usually hurt conversions quietly. They do not always look broken, but they do fail to move visitors toward action.

## Mobile QA on real devices

Do not rely on desktop-only review.

Use at least one real phone and check:

- text size and readability
- spacing around buttons and forms
- sticky headers or floating UI that may block content
- tap target size for calls, WhatsApp buttons, and menus
- image crop behavior
- horizontal overflow
- section stacking order
- form usability

Many launch problems are only obvious when you test the live page as a real visitor would.

## Forms and lead-capture QA

Form testing is not complete until the live domain is tested.

Verify:

- the form submits successfully
- the correct inbox receives the message
- spam protection does not block legitimate submissions
- auto-response or confirmation behavior works as intended
- required fields and validation messages are clear
- mobile users can complete the form without friction
- submission tracking fires where relevant

If the business depends on calls or WhatsApp more than forms, test those paths just as seriously.

## SEO and crawlability QA

Launch-day SEO mistakes are often preventable.

Check the following before go-live:

- title and meta description exist for the homepage and primary money pages
- canonical URLs match the live preferred version
- noindex is not accidentally applied to indexable pages
- redirects exist for replaced URLs
- sitemap and robots routes are accessible
- internal links do not point to staging or broken routes
- Open Graph image and metadata are present on key pages

These are not just technical niceties. They affect how the site is crawled, indexed, and presented from the beginning.

## Trust and compliance QA

Trust pages are part of launch readiness, not optional extras.

Before launch, confirm that the site has accessible, substantial versions of:

- About
- Contact
- Privacy Policy
- Terms
- Disclaimer
- Editorial Policy

Also check that:

- pricing or scope language is not misleading
- case studies do not make unverified claims
- there is no obvious placeholder text
- the site purpose is clear
- ads or monetization surfaces do not crowd primary content

This matters for credibility with users and for broader AdSense-readiness discipline.

## Analytics and tracking QA

Tracking should be tested before launch and again after launch.

At minimum, verify:

- GA4 page views fire correctly
- main conversion events are recorded
- call-click or WhatsApp-click events work where configured
- thank-you pages or success states are measurable
- tag-manager or pixel changes are not blocked by missing consent or broken triggers

If the site launches without reliable measurement, it becomes much harder to diagnose whether the problem is traffic, trust, or conversion.

For deeper implementation work, use the [website tracking setup for small businesses](/blog/website-tracking-setup-for-small-businesses/).

## Performance QA

You do not need a perfect synthetic score to launch well, but you do need a page that feels stable and usable.

Check for:

- oversized hero imagery
- delayed text rendering
- layout shift
- heavy above-the-fold scripts
- slow mobile rendering on real devices
- broken lazy-loading behavior

For a small business site, performance is part of brand quality. A page that feels slow can make the business feel less trustworthy before the visitor even reads the offer.

## Launch-day timeline

Use a simple timeline instead of improvising:

1. `T-24h`: final staging QA and final approvals
2. `T-4h`: confirm backups, deployment plan, rollback notes, and redirect sheet
3. `T-0h`: deploy or switch DNS
4. `T+15m`: smoke test homepage, top service pages, contact form, and primary CTA path
5. `T+1h`: confirm tracking, redirects, sitemap, and robots behavior
6. `T+24h`: review analytics, enquiry flow, and any early technical regressions
7. `T+72h`: run a short post-launch audit for broken links, mobile issues, and conversion friction

This creates accountability and removes avoidable launch chaos.

## Post-launch monitoring checklist

The first 72 hours matter more than many teams expect.

Monitor:

- enquiry volume and inbox delivery
- 404 errors or redirect misses
- mobile layout breakage
- analytics event consistency
- performance complaints
- crawl-visible content issues
- unexpected noindex or canonical problems

If a launch issue appears, write down:

- what failed
- when it failed
- who owns the fix
- whether a rollback is needed

That turns firefighting into controlled response.

## Who should own what

Launches often fail because ownership is vague.

Before go-live, decide:

- who gives final approval
- who watches the inbox
- who checks analytics
- who verifies redirects
- who handles urgent fixes
- who communicates with the client or stakeholders if something slips

Many launch failures are communication failures dressed up as technical problems.

## Final note

Launch QA is not optional polish.

It is the last serious chance to protect trust, lead flow, and crawl quality before the public sees the new site. A clean launch compounds confidence. A messy launch compounds support work, lost trust, and preventable confusion.
